Research & background

Connecting processes.
Understanding patterns.

My work combines atmospheric physics with statistical learning to study severe-weather environments and climate extremes.

How do atmospheric environments shape the extremes we experience—and how might those environments change?

I investigate this question using reanalysis, climate-model output, weather simulations, station measurements, and hazard reports. My M.S. research at Western Kentucky University focuses on severe-storm environments in the Ohio River Basin, with related projects extending to heat, drought, and precipitation extremes.

Working with Xingang Fan, Zachary J. Suriano, and Manmeet Singh, I develop reproducible workflows for environmental diagnostics, event–environment matching, model calibration, and independent verification.

01

Severe weather

From environments

ERA5 diagnostics, NOAA storm reports, Random Forest, and XGBoost support hazard-specific estimates for tornadoes, severe hail, and severe thunderstorm winds.

Historical observations reveal where extremes intensify together. I examine long-term change alongside sensitivity to thresholds, baselines, and drought definitions.
