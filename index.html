About

Atmospheric science across scales.

I am an atmospheric scientist at Western Kentucky University specializing in severe-storm environments, climate variability, and prediction frameworks. I combine physical understanding of the atmosphere with high-performance computing and machine learning to quantify how storm-supportive environments vary across space and time and how they may change in future climates.

01

Severe-weather environments

My current research focuses on the Ohio River Basin, where northward moisture transport from the Gulf of Mexico helps create conditions favorable for deep convection. I use ERA5 reanalysis, the NOAA Storm Events Database, and downscaled CMIP6 projections to study tornado, severe-hail, and damaging-wind environments at three-hour intervals.

Random Forest and XGBoost models connect reported events with 107 thermodynamic, kinematic, moisture, and composite diagnostics, including CAPE, vertical wind shear, storm-relative helicity, and lapse rates.

02

High-performance computing

I process multi-terabyte atmospheric datasets on NCAR Derecho and Casper with Dr. Xingang Fan and on TACC Stampede3 with Dr. Manmeet Singh. These workflows produce multi-decadal climatologies and reproducible data pipelines using Python, xarray, MetPy, scikit-learn, NetCDF, Bash, and PBS scheduling.

03

Numerical modeling and broader interests

I also use the Weather Research and Forecasting model to simulate mesoscale convective systems, flash floods, and other high-impact events. These simulations provide process-level context for the statistical analysis. My broader interests include severe weather, climate modeling, hydroclimatology, and climate dynamics.
